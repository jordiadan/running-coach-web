# Design System: Running Coach

## Overview

Baseline capture of the code at commit `46c8658`, with a scoped authenticated
portal update from `portal.css`, `weekly-plan.css`, `PortalPage.tsx`, Connect and
Profile on 2026-10-16. Working conventions live in AGENTS.md and product context
in PRODUCT.md. No canonical Pencil frame is tracked; this record does not claim
a comparison with Pencil. Observable sources are `src/index.css`,
`tailwind.config.ts`, `src/components/ui/` and their consuming screens.

The implementation combines serif headings, sans-serif text, tonal surfaces,
soft borders and rounded controls. The landing page includes running photography
with overlays and motion; the portal uses lists, metrics and session cards.
A screen's composition is not promoted to a global rule.

Frontmatter tokens record existing values, with warm as the base theme and
prefixes for the other declared variants. They index the source rather than
authorize changes. Check differences against the code and project instructions
before updating this record.

The authenticated extension carries Weekly Plan's existing type, theme roles and
geometry into Connect, Profile and their shared shell. Its brief is
`docs/portal-design-iteration.md`; it introduces no public-page direction or
rebrand. Global tokens and unrelated baseline observations remain as recorded;
this update does not claim to reconcile all pre-existing documentation drift.

The onboarding extension follows `docs/onboarding-design-iteration.md`, using
the same portal roles, typography and shared Connect/Profile controls. Its
setup rail and plan-status receipt are scoped to first-run tasks; they introduce
no new theme, tokens, dependencies or raster assets.

## Colors

### Primary

The base theme uses muted green `primary` for actions and emphasis, with
`primary-foreground` for content over that background. `ring` follows the
primary color for focus. Frontmatter preserves the source HSL values.

### Secondary

`secondary` and `secondary-foreground` serve secondary controls and backgrounds.
`muted` and `muted-foreground` distinguish supporting content. `accent` is the
warm accent used in details such as key-session indicators; no fixed color
distribution is established.

### Neutral

`background` and `foreground` define the document; `card` and `popover` separate
surfaces. `border`, `input` and `divider` have distinct roles even where their
values coincide. `surface-elevated` and `text-subtle` have their own utilities.
`destructive` and its foreground represent errors and destructive actions.

`src/index.css` declares Warm & Earthy, Dark & Bold, Clean & Bright and Forest &
Deep. `useTheme.tsx` defaults to warm and stores the selection in `rc-theme`.
The frontmatter's `dark-`, `bright-` and `forest-` prefixes describe those
variants, not independent colors to mix on a screen. The initial setup's computed
style capture confirmed the base theme. Its compiled CSS contained `.theme-warm`
but not the other three selectors. The portal iteration fixes that compatibility
defect with literal theme class names in `useTheme.tsx`, allowing Tailwind to
retain all four declared themes without changing their palettes. The historical
capture does not establish current theme rendering or contrast compliance.

The portal inherits foreground, background, surface-elevated, secondary, border,
primary and primary-foreground roles. Supporting text uses foreground at 70%
opacity; field borders use foreground at 45%. Its action pair follows the theme:
the warm theme supplies the forest-green active-source surface, rather than a
new fixed palette. Scoped contrast adjustments darken the dark theme's action
fill with 40% black. Bright mixes its primary text/focus role with 15% black and
its action fill with 30% black. Error text mixes destructive with 10% black on
light themes; dark and forest use `hsl(0 85% 75%)`. These overrides live in
`portal.css` and do not replace the global theme tokens. Weekly Plan consumes
these same primary, action and error roles, keeping
colored surfaces separate from primary text and focus. Their presence alone is
not a contrast-compliance verification.

## Typography

DM Serif Display with Georgia/serif is used for headings and the brand; DM Sans
with system-ui/sans-serif for body text and controls. They load from Google Fonts
in `src/index.css`; no local proprietary fonts are documented.

Frontmatter captures common roles rather than every Tailwind size. The landing
h1 is 3rem, 4.5rem from sm and 6rem from lg, with tight tracking.
`leading-[1.05]` is declared there, but `text-7xl`/`text-8xl` produce line-height
1 in the initially inspected compiled CSS. That setup's computed capture at
390px was 48px/50.4px and at 1440px was 96px/96px. Do not translate this combination
into a new `clamp()` as part of documentation.

Weekly Plan, Connect, Profile and onboarding task headings use the portal-heading
role, switching to portal-heading-wide from 640px (40px / 48px). Connect and Profile
supporting copy commonly uses 13–15px; editable fields use 16px. Portal loading
and authentication states retain their local heading sizes. The onboarding
desktop rail title and plan-summary heading use 28px serif type. The
landing also uses `text-lg`/`text-xl`. Session metrics use tabular numbers.
There is no global body-text width limit in ch; do not impose one by default.

## Layout

Mobile-first layout uses Tailwind's responsive utilities. Landing containers use
`max-w-6xl` (72rem); privacy uses `max-w-2xl` (42rem); sign-in uses
`max-w-sm` (24rem). The most common horizontal padding is `px-6` (24px).
Spacing follows Tailwind's scale, with frequent steps captured in frontmatter
and screen-specific values retained in their consumers.

Default breakpoints are sm (640px), md (768px), lg (1024px), xl (1280px) and
2xl (1536px). Tailwind's `container` config sets a 1400px width at 2xl; that is
not the breakpoint. Sign-in shows its side panel from lg; Profile essentials
use two columns from sm. Template components in `src/components/ui/`
do not establish a global sidebar or a multicolumn dashboard.

The authenticated header and main content, including onboarding, share a 72rem
maximum, with 24px horizontal padding. Below 900px, the single set of
three destination links forms a fixed bottom bar with safe-area padding; from
900px it sits in the sticky header. Main content leaves room for the bottom bar.
The header keeps theme and logout controls available at both sizes.

Onboarding has no destination bar. Below 1024px, a labelled three-step sequence
sits above one task heading and its controls. From 1024px, a sticky 14rem setup
rail sits beside the task with a 4rem gap. Shared provider surfaces remain
compact and stacked below 640px, then use two columns; the onboarding task
omits Connect's settings guidance column and Profile's identity/zones aside.
The plan receipt stacks summary and status on mobile; from 1024px, its summary
and receipt fields share a two-column row above the status region.

Connect stacks provider surfaces on narrow screens, places them side by side
from 640px, and adds a 16rem guidance column from 900px. Profile is one column
below 900px, with compact identity above the editor; from 900px the editor leads
beside a 17.5rem identity/zones aside. Profile running-day controls use four
columns below 640px (56px minimum height; at least 44px wide at a 320px viewport),
then seven columns with 80px minimum height. Weekly Plan's date navigator uses
four columns below 400px to retain 44px-wide targets, then seven columns. Weekly
Plan retains its 1024px workspace breakpoint.

## Elevation & Depth

The implementation uses tonal surfaces, borders, Tailwind shadows and blurred
navigation. Base cards use `shadow-sm`; raised or open controls can use larger
shadows. Screens reference `shadow-card`, but the inspected CSS and config define
no custom token for it; do not invent a value. Photo overlays and gradients,
Today cards and their states are observed behavior rather than a universal recipe.

CSS animates body color changes over 0.3s. The portal uses opacity and translation
transitions, springs and expansions; some screens consult `useReducedMotion`.
The landing uses staggered entrance motion and parallax. This record does not
claim application-wide reduced-motion coverage.

Portal provider/editor surfaces use a restrained shadow
(`0 8px 24px -16px hsl(var(--foreground) / 0.22)`). Settings controls and navigation
use 160ms state transitions; shell destination changes use 160ms opacity and 4px
translation. Scoped reduced-motion rules remove portal animations, transitions
and active-state translation, and the shell consults `useReducedMotion`.

## Shapes

The base radius variable is 0.625rem; `lg` uses it and `md`/`sm` subtract 2px/4px.
Bright declares 0.75rem. Portal cards also use Tailwind's `rounded-xl` and
`rounded-2xl`; chips and indicators use circular shapes. These classes do not
all depend on the radius variable. Preserve the distinction between semantic
radii and component-specific radii.

The portal extension repeats 12px control and 16px surface radii, following
Weekly Plan. Profile day controls use 10px corners; identity monograms and small
selection indicators remain circular. These scoped values do not change the
base UI library's radius variable.

## Components

### Buttons

`src/components/ui/button.tsx` defines default, destructive, outline, secondary,
ghost, link, hero and hero-outline. Base height is 40px; sm is 36px; lg is 44px.
Hero retains the primary color and uses base text with wide tracking.
Outline/ghost retain their existing states.

Focus uses a 2px ring with a 2px offset. Disabled reduces opacity and blocks
interaction. `asChild` composes the consumer's element; verify semantics when
reusing it. Some CTAs have local size overrides.

Portal account controls retain 44px targets. Provider actions are at least 46px
high; Profile save and editable-field controls are at least 48px high. Portal
focus uses a 2px primary outline with a 4px offset. These local sizes supplement
the base Button variants rather than redefining every button as 40px.

### Cards / Containers

The base Card has lg radius, a border, card background, card-foreground text and
shadow-sm; internal slots generally use 24px padding. Portal-specific cards
vary with 16px/20px/24px padding and xl/2xl radii. Documentation preserves them.

### Inputs / Fields

The base Input uses input border, background fill, 40px height, 12px/8px padding
and md radius. Base text switches to sm from md; focus has a 2px ring and offset.
Profile composes existing labels, selects and calendar/popover controls. Errors
use destructive text and operations can disable controls.

The Profile editor overrides base inputs, selects, comboboxes and date controls
to the portal-input geometry, with ground fill and the scoped control border.
Read-only fields use the soft surface; pending saves disable the editing
fieldset. Selection, validation and calculated zones remain distinct states.

### Navigation

Serif brand with sans-serif links and controls. The landing has fixed navigation
with transparency and blur; the portal uses a sticky header and Weekly Plan,
Connect and Profile destination links, placed as described in Layout. Links
retain native URL and history behavior through the `view` query parameter.
The current destination has an underline, stronger weight and tonal fill as well
as color. A skip link targets main content. The theme selector is a Radix
dropdown. Its availability does not establish a new navigation pattern for other
pages.

### Connect

The active provider is first and uses the theme's action surface and contrasting
text. Each provider keeps identity, status and capability before its explicit
Connect, Reconnect, Replace source or Disconnect action. The inactive source is
quiet. Disconnect and replacement use confirmation dialogs. Loading, unavailable
status/retry, pending authorization and operation failures retain visible feedback.
The provider composition is scoped to this screen, not a generic card template.

Portal confirmation dialogs carry the same scoped roles outside the shell's DOM
subtree. Their surfaces use 16px corners, buttons use 12px corners and 48px minimum
height, and confirmation buttons use the theme-adjusted action pair. Supporting
and destructive text use the portal's corresponding roles. The dialogs preserve
the portal focus outline and suppress dialog/overlay animation and transitions
under reduced motion.

### Profile

One editor groups essentials, weekly rhythm and race goal, with identity and
calculated zones as supporting context. Running-day buttons expose pressed state;
the four-day minimum and long-run select remain visible constraints. The save bar
becomes sticky only while dirty, saving or failed; its resting/success state stays
in flow. On mobile it clears the bottom navigation, and on desktop it sits 16px
above the viewport bottom. Saving, unsaved, error and success feedback share the
same region.

Dirty state compares the persisted profile fields and selected race target;
display-only time/pace equivalents do not count as edits. Successful saves update
the saved baseline even when the following read returns an unchanged snapshot.

Unsaved edits live in shell memory, including time/pace drafts, so Back/Forward
between portal destinations can restore them. Explicit navigation away and
logout offer discard confirmation; document unload uses the browser warning.
There is no draft storage persistence. This behavior documents the shipped flow,
not a new backend contract or a promise of recovery after reload.

### Onboarding

One task title leads the shared Connect or Profile controls without their
settings-page headings. The labelled sequence exposes current, completed and
upcoming steps; desktop adds descriptions beside the numbered/check indicators.
Compact mobile providers preserve source identity, capability and authorization
feedback. Profile retains validation, weekly rhythm, conditional race details
and save feedback, while omitting the future coach-notes placeholder.

The first-plan surface uses a definition-list receipt for the connected training
source, saved profile and weekly-plan status, derived from bootstrap facts.
Missing, preparing, failed and ready remain distinct states: missing says the
first plan is not available yet, preparing reports preparation, failure preserves
setup and offers retry, and ready offers one explicit Open weekly plan action.
It does not show simulated generation milestones or percentages. Refresh and
retry errors stay inline; background bootstrap failure retains the last loaded
step. Error details are available through a native disclosure when supplied.

An onboarding transition to ready waits for that explicit action before showing
the actual weekly plan. A first bootstrap that is already ready enters the
portal directly. Step changes focus the task heading; entry focuses portal main
content. Brief opacity/translation changes and the checking spinner respect
reduced motion. Onboarding reuses shell-memory profile drafts and leave/logout
protection; its active mobile save bar clears the safe area without reserving
space for a destination bar.

### Weekly Plan

Screen-specific composition in `src/components/portal/WeeklyPlanScreen.tsx`:
week navigation, metrics, goal state and sessions. Today distinguishes today's
session and completion. Matched activities show provenance and Strava or
Intervals links when the contract supplies them. The matched activity remains
visible when manual completion changes; evidence and the checkbox are independent
states. Do not extract modality colors, intensities or the entire composition
into shared components without actual repetition.

## Do's and Don'ts

- **Do** reuse existing CSS roles and variants before adding values.
- **Do** preserve observed assets, typography and content when refining.
- **Do** follow AGENTS.md and define a mobile-first hierarchy before implementing
  new screens or structural changes; compare with the agreed design or brief.
- **Don't** treat this capture as an approved rebrand, a UX audit or accessibility
  certification for every theme.
- **Don't** create components from templates the product does not yet use.
- **Don't** invent shadows, percentage rules, a visual concept or a preferred
  final theme that the sources do not establish.
